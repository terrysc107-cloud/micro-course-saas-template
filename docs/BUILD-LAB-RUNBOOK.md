# The Build Lab — facilitator run book

**Founding cohort.** Four Wednesdays: **Nov 18, Dec 2, Dec 9, Dec 16, 2026.**
7:00pm ET. Online. Eight seats. $997, course and Kit included.

This is the document you run the sessions from. It is not marketing copy and it
is not the curriculum the students read; they read the lessons. This is what you
do in the room.

---

## 1. The one rule

**Live time never teaches what the lessons already teach.**

The board path is already written, already has per-lesson quizzes, and already
costs $57. If a session delivers that content again out loud, the Lab is a
slower version of the course and the room will feel it by week two. The lessons
are pre-work. Every minute of live time goes to one of the four things text
cannot do:

1. Look at **their** board and say what is wrong with it.
2. Show a decision being made in real time, **including the ones that go wrong**.
3. Put eight people who all committed to shipping in the same room.
4. Answer "but my business is different", out loud, with their actual business
   on screen.

If you catch yourself explaining a concept the lesson covers, stop and say
"that's lesson 20/02, read it tonight" and go back to their screen. That
sentence is not rudeness, it is the product working.

### What this is not

**No slide deck.** We have a good deck pipeline and it is the wrong tool here.
A deck pulls the room toward lecture, and lecture is the failure mode this
format exists to avoid. One agenda card per session is enough. The artifact in
this subject is a screen with a folder on it.

**No graded quiz.** The certification course we run elsewhere is quizzed
because the deliverable there is recall for a standardized exam. Nothing here
is examined. The check is binary and real: *did your board produce a meeting
this week?* Re-quizzing adults on material they already have quizzes for tests
the wrong thing and reads as condescending at this price.

**No certificate.** They leave with a board, which is better and also true.

## 2. Prerequisites

$997 includes the course and the Operating Company Kit. That is not generosity,
it is the pre-work gate: **everyone must arrive at session 1 with module
`00-start-here` finished and a board that has run once.**

Without the gate, session 1 becomes group tech support for the two people who
have not installed anything, while six people who did the work watch. That is
how a cohort loses its room in the first hour.

**Send the pre-work email at booking and again seven days out.** It asks for
exactly three things:

1. Finish `00-start-here`.
2. Have your folder with `GOALS.md`, `METRICS.md`, `DECISION-LOG.md` and
   `PIPELINE.md` in it, with your real numbers, however rough.
3. Run it once, and bring the output, even if the output is bad. **Especially
   if the output is bad.**

Expect roughly one person in eight to arrive without it anyway. Session 1 has a
triage block for exactly that person. After session 1 there is no more triage;
they pair with someone.

## 3. The session template

Same shape every week, so the rhythm gets familiar fast and nobody has to ask
what happens next.

| Block | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Show and tell** | 15-20 min | 2-3 people share screen: here is my board, here is what it produced. Rotating, so everyone presents once across the four weeks. |
| **You build live** | 20-25 min | On your own board, on your real business. The part that cannot be written down. |
| **Work block** | 50-65 min | They build. Cameras on, screens shared. You move between them. This is the product. |
| **Blockers and commitments** | 15-20 min | Each person names what will run before next session. |

The work block is the biggest block on purpose. If a session runs long, **cut
your live build, never the work block.** Your demo is replaceable; an hour with
you looking at their screen is the thing they paid for.

**Session 1 is three hours. Sessions 2 to 4 are two hours.** Going from an empty
folder to something that runs is the heaviest lift of the four and the one where
somebody's laptop will fight them. The `ccc_lab_sessions` row carries a single
`duration_minutes` (120), so session 1's extra hour lives here and in the
calendar invite, not in the schema.

## 4. Session 1 — Wed Nov 18, 3 hours

### Your first board

**They arrive with:** module 00 done, four files with real numbers, one run.
**They leave with:** a board that runs *on a schedule*, and the ability to tell
a useful run from a plausible one.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome. The one rule, the four weeks, and what the gap in the middle is for. |
| 0:10 | **Live build.** Start a board from nothing on a real business. Fill the four files in front of them. |
| 0:35 | **Work block 1.** Everyone's four files become real. Triage anyone who arrived cold. |
| 1:25 | Break, 10 min. |
| 1:35 | **Live build.** The schedule. How a run happens on a day nobody sits down. |
| 1:55 | **Work block 2.** Everyone schedules their run. |
| 2:40 | Commitments. Assign show-and-tell slots. Explain the gap. |

### The moment that matters

In the first live build, **run it once and get a confidently wrong answer on
purpose**, then show how you caught it. A plausible number looks exactly like a
real one, and the whole rest of the cohort depends on them being able to see the
difference in week one. If you do not show them a bad run now, they will trust
their own bad runs for three weeks.

Say the line plainly: *if a number you need is missing, the board must say it is
missing and not estimate it.*

### Closing the session

The gap is two weeks. Frame it as the assignment it is, not as a holiday:

> "Your board runs without you until Dec 2. Do not babysit it. On Dec 2 we open
> with what died, and something will have died. That is the point."

## 5. Session 2 — Wed Dec 2, 2 hours

### What died, and the four files that fix it

**They arrive with:** two weeks of unattended running.
**They leave with:** the memory tiers, `README.md` as the index the board reads
first, and `memory/CANDIDATES.md`.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Show and tell**, 3 people. The question is literally "what did your board do for two weeks?" |
| 0:20 | **Live build.** Your own silent failure and the diagnosis. |
| 0:40 | **Work block.** Memory tiers, the index, the inbox that drains, `CANDIDATES.md`. |
| 1:45 | Blockers and commitments. |

### The moment that matters

This is the best session of the four and it is the one a single-day format
cannot buy. Tell them about the run of yours that stopped and went **40 days**
before anyone noticed. A job that stops does not error. It produces nothing, and
nothing looks exactly like a quiet week.

Then connect it to what they just lived: whoever's board went quiet over
Thanksgiving now has a two-week-old example of the failure in their own
business, which is worth more than any explanation.

Expect one of three outcomes per person, and have a response ready for each:

- **It ran and was useful.** Ask what it got wrong. There will be something.
- **It ran and produced confident nonsense.** The best case for teaching. Trace
  one claim back to its source, live, and let the room watch it fail to have one.
- **It stopped.** The lesson landed. Ask them how they found out, and how long
  it took.

## 6. Session 3 — Wed Dec 9, 2 hours

### Seats and the meeting

**They arrive with:** memory in place, one run since Dec 2.
**They leave with:** `CHARTER.md` for their first seat, `PROMOTION-LADDER.md`,
and a weekly meeting format that is theirs.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Show and tell**, 3 people. |
| 0:15 | **Live build.** Write a charter from scratch, out loud. |
| 0:40 | **Work block.** Charter, promotion ladder, first real meeting. |
| 1:45 | Blockers and commitments. |

### The moment that matters

A prompt is what you ask. A charter is who it is. The line that does the work is
the refusal: **name something the seat will refuse to do, even when it would
rather not.** Make every person in the room write that line before they leave,
and read three of them out.

Second thing to land: goals are **floors**, not targets. A board given targets
reports that everything looks fine, every week, forever, because nothing can be
off track. Most people arrive having written targets. Have them convert one.

## 7. Session 4 — Wed Dec 16, 2 hours

### Real data and the review loop

**They arrive with:** a chartered seat and at least one meeting it produced.
**They leave with:** one metric wired to a real system of record, the query
recorded beside the number, and a loop that improves the board instead of just
growing it.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Show and tell**, the remaining 2 people. |
| 0:15 | **Live build.** Give a seat a read-only window onto a real source. |
| 0:40 | **Work block.** Everyone wires one real number. One is enough. |
| 1:35 | The review loop: candidates, promotion by evidence, corrections as output. |
| 1:50 | What to do in January. Close. |

### The moment that matters

Hand-typed numbers rot silently, and a rotted number is indistinguishable from a
live one until it costs you something. **One** real connection is the goal, not
five. The habit that matters is recording the query next to the number, so a
future reader can check it instead of trusting it.

### Closing the cohort

Do not end on a summary. End on the next four weeks: what runs, what they check,
and the one thing each person is going to wire up next. Then tell them plainly
what the Board Room is and let them decide later. Nobody is sold to in the last
ten minutes of something they already paid for.

## 8. Show and tell

Eight people, three sessions of it: **3 on Dec 2, 3 on Dec 9, 2 on Dec 16.**
Everyone goes exactly once. Assign the slots at the end of session 1 so people
can prepare and nobody is ambushed.

Three questions, same every time, on the agenda card:

1. What did your board produce?
2. What did it get wrong?
3. What are you stuck on?

**Question 2 is the one that makes this work.** A show and tell where everything
went well teaches nobody anything and quietly pressures the next person to
perform. Go first yourself in session 2 with something of yours that failed, and
the honesty holds for the rest of the cohort.

### Sensitive numbers

Some of these people will not want their revenue on eight strangers' screens,
and they are right not to. **Structure, not values, is always an acceptable
share.** Say so out loud in session 1, before anyone has to ask for it. A blurred
number costs the room nothing; a person who skips their slot because they felt
cornered costs it a lot.

## 9. Recording

**Record the first 35 minutes only.** Show and tell plus your live build. Stop
the recording before the work block, out loud, so the room hears it stop.

The ladder promises recordings and the working files afterward, and this
delivers that. What it refuses to deliver is eight people's real revenue,
pipeline and customer names in a file we then hand to seven other people. The
work block is where those are on screen, and it is also the least watchable
footage imaginable.

- Get show-and-tell consent at booking, in writing, and again in the room.
- Anyone who declines still presents; you just stop the recording for their
  slot.
- Publish recordings to the cohort only, not to the course.

---

## 10. Between sessions

**An async thread. No SLA, stated up front.**

People post what their board produced. You answer when you can. Saying "no
guaranteed response time" at the start costs nothing and protects you from
having quietly sold four weeks of on-demand consulting for $997.

The thread's real job is the two-week gap, which is where cohorts lose people.
An assigned show-and-tell slot plus a thread with some activity in it is usually
enough to keep all eight through Dec 2.

**Do not run office hours in the gap week.** It partly defeats the unattended
run, which is the thing session 2 is built on.

## 11. What goes wrong

| Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|
| Someone arrives at session 1 with nothing done | Triage inside work block 1. After session 1, they pair with someone rather than getting your time one-on-one. |
| A board produces beautiful nonsense | Best teaching moment available. Trace one claim to its source live and let it fail in front of everyone. |
| Silence in the work block | Cameras on is the structural fix. Then rotate: name a person, ask what is on their screen right now. |
| The room wants a lecture | They are asking for the thing that is already written down. Point at the lesson and go back to their screen. |
| Somebody drops after the gap | Their show-and-tell slot is the hook. Message them personally before Dec 2, not after. |
| A session runs long | Cut your live build. Never the work block. |
| Fewer than 4 sold by Oct 21 | Move the date. A cohort of one is worse than a cohort that slipped. |
