Most people treat agent autonomy as a switch. Either you approve everything, or
you turn approvals off and hope.

Both are bad. Approving everything means the board only runs when you are
watching, which defeats the point. Turning approvals off means a thing you have
not evaluated is acting on your business.

The real question is never "do I trust it." It is **"which specific actions has
this seat earned."** That is a ladder.

## Four ranks

Each rung names what it unlocks and what stays gated.

**L1 Operator.** Reads everything, drafts anything, writes to its own board
files. Publishes nothing, sends nothing, spends nothing. Everything starts here.

**L2 Executive.** Can act on the low-stakes reversible things it has been
recommending correctly for months. Still cannot send or spend.

**L3 Managing Director.** Can act unsupervised inside a defined lane. Sends the
things you have read the drafts of forty times.

**L4 Chief Executive.** Discretionary budget within a cap.

**And permanent limits, at every rank, forever:** strategy ratification, major
spend, pricing philosophy, legal, and any decision to sell or wind down. These
are not on the ladder. A seat at the top rung still does not touch them, and
saying so in the file is what makes the rest of the ladder safe to climb.

## The seat asks. You grant.

This is the part that makes it work.

**The seat requests a promotion, citing evidence.** You review. You grant or you
hold. Promotions are revocable.

The evidence comes from the decision log, which is why every entry there is
stamped with the rank it was made under. That column turns "I have been doing
well" into something checkable: here are eleven decisions made at L1, here is
what happened to each.

Criteria should be boring and countable:

```
L1 -> L2 requires all of:
- [ ] 8 consecutive weekly reports that were useful and accurate
- [ ] Zero published numbers later found wrong
- [ ] Zero guardrail violations
- [ ] A recommendation you acted on that worked
```

Checkboxes, not judgement calls. You want to be able to disagree with the
request on facts.

Put all of this in **`PROMOTION-LADDER.md`**, beside your charter. Two files
rather than one because they change at different rates: a rank moves on
promotion, and a charter almost never moves at all.

## Make surfacing mandatory

Add a required step to your weekly playbook: **state the rank, state whether you
are requesting a promotion, and if not, say why not.**

This exists because a seat that never asks never gets what it needs, and then
quietly underperforms for reasons nobody wrote down. Silence here is a failure
mode, not modesty.

The output is often better than a request. Here is a real one, from a seat
declining to ask:

> **Not requesting L3.** L3 needs a clean quarter at L2 plus a playbook that
> runs unattended. The daily run has been dead 40 days and paid accounts are
> falling. Those fail the last two criteria.

That seat just told its owner the two most important problems in the business,
in the course of explaining why it was not asking for a raise.

## Why this is worth the effort

Three things fall out of it that you do not get any other way.

**Trust becomes incremental.** You are never deciding whether to trust the
board. You are deciding whether one specific new permission is warranted, on
evidence.

**Failure becomes cheap.** A seat that gets something wrong at L1 wrote a bad
draft. The same mistake at L4 spent money. The ladder means capability arrives
after the track record, not before.

**You get an honest self-assessment for free.** The step that forces a seat to
justify its rank makes it audit its own performance, and it will name problems
you were avoiding.

## Start at L1 and stay there a while

Your first seat should draft and nothing else, for at least a month.

Not because it will misbehave, but because you do not yet know what it is like
when it is wrong. That is the thing you are actually evaluating, and you can
only learn it by reading drafts of things that were never sent.

<KeyPoint>Autonomy is a ladder, not a switch. The seat requests and cites its decision log, you grant or hold, and promotions are revocable. Some decisions never move regardless of rank. Make surfacing mandatory, because a seat explaining why it is not asking will tell you what is actually broken.</KeyPoint>
