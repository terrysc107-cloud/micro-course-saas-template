Your board will produce something articulate every single time. Articulate is
free. Useful is not.

This lesson is the filter. It takes under a minute per run and it is the
difference between a board you come to trust and one you quietly stop opening.

## Check 1: can you trace every claim?

For each statement, ask: **which number in my files is this from?**

Good:

> Enquiries are at 4 against a floor of 8 (METRICS.md), and the last two both
> came from the same referral source, so the channel mix has not actually
> widened.

Traceable. You can open the file and check it.

Bad:

> Your lead generation is underperforming relative to industry benchmarks and
> there is significant room for optimisation.

What number? Whose benchmarks? This is a sentence shaped like an insight. It
would be equally true of any business, which means it is telling you nothing
about yours.

## Check 2: did it admit what it did not have?

A good run says so:

> Website visits are listed as unknown in METRICS.md, so I cannot tell whether
> the enquiry drop is a traffic problem or a conversion problem. That is the
> next number worth getting.

**That is the single most valuable line a board can produce.** It found the
gap, told you the gap matters, and told you why.

If your runs never mention missing data, and your files contain `unknown`
anywhere, the seat is filling gaps instead of reporting them. Fix the brief
before you read another word of the output.

## Check 3: does it contradict your decision log?

If a run recommends something you already decided against, the seat did not
read `DECISION-LOG.md`.

That is a brief problem, not a judgement problem. Name the file explicitly and
it stops happening. Until you do, you will re-argue the same three questions
every month, which is precisely what the file exists to prevent.

## Check 4: could you act on it today?

Actionable:

> Follow up with Ravi. Quote sent 11 days ago, no reply, and PIPELINE.md says
> your next step was Thursday.

Not actionable:

> Consider strengthening your follow-up processes.

The second is not wrong. It is just not a thing you can do, and a board that
produces a stream of unarguable non-actions is a board you will stop reading
by week three.

## The failure that is hardest to see

**Everything is fine.**

Run after run comes back broadly positive. Nothing is flagged. It feels like
things are going well.

Usually it means the seat has nothing to compare against. Without floors in
`GOALS.md`, no number can be off track, because there is no line to be under.
Everything is fine because nothing can be not fine.

Go back and write floors. This is the most common reason a board feels
pleasant and useless.

## Grade a run in one minute

| Ask | If no |
|---|---|
| Every claim traces to a file? | Name the files in the brief |
| Missing data mentioned? | Add the do-not-estimate line |
| Consistent with past decisions? | Name `DECISION-LOG.md` in the brief |
| Could you act on it today? | Ask for named next actions, not themes |

Three of the four fixes are one line in the brief. The board is rarely the
problem.

## Prefer short and specific

A run that names two things and cites the numbers behind them beats one that
covers everything. You are not looking for a report. You are looking for the
two things you would not have noticed.

Tell your seats that directly, and they will do it.

<KeyPoint>Trace every claim to a file, expect missing data to be named, check it against your decisions, and require actions you could take today. When a run is useless, the brief is usually the cause. When every run says things are fine, you have no floors.</KeyPoint>
