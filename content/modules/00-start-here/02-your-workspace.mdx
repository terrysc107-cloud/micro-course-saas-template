> **The next lesson hands you all of this pre-made.** Read this one anyway. It
> is what each file is *for*, and these four are the ones you will be editing
> every week for as long as the board runs.

Every guide to this kind of tool starts by telling you to open your project.

If you do not write software, you do not have one, and that sentence quietly
ends the tutorial. So let us deal with it first.

## Your project is your business

A "project" is just **a folder with files in it**. Software people keep code in
theirs. You are going to keep your business in yours.

That is not a workaround. It is the same thing. The tool reads files and writes
files; it was never picky about what the files contained.

## Step 1: make the folder

Wherever you keep documents, make a new folder and name it `ai-board`.

Somewhere you will actually find it again. Your Documents folder is fine. Your
desktop is fine. If you sync with Dropbox, iCloud, or Drive, inside that is
fine and gives you version history for free.

That is the whole step.

## Step 2: make four files

Inside `ai-board`, create four plain text files. Any text editor works. On a
Mac, TextEdit in plain-text mode. On Windows, Notepad. If you have a markdown
app like Obsidian or Bear, even better, but nothing here requires one.

The `.md` on the end means markdown, which is a fancy way of saying "text with
a few conventions for headings and lists." You already know enough of it.

### `GOALS.md`

```
# Goals

## This quarter
- Revenue: floor $4,000/month. Currently around $2,600.
- New enquiries: floor 8/month. Currently 3 or 4.

## What I am NOT doing this quarter
- No new service lines. Depth over breadth until revenue is stable.

## How I will know it worked
- Three months in a row at or above the floor, not one good month.
```

Write floors, not dreams. A floor is the lowest number you would accept without
concluding the quarter went badly. Floors make a seat's advice concrete;
aspirations make it vague.

### `METRICS.md`

```
# Metrics

Last updated: (put today's date here)

| Metric | Value | Source | As of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly revenue | $2,640 | Stripe dashboard, month to date | Mar 03 |
| Enquiries this month | 4 | Contact form inbox, counted by hand | Mar 03 |
| Email list | 212 | Mailchimp, audience tab | Mar 01 |
| Website visits (30d) | unknown | No analytics installed yet | - |
```

**The last two columns are the important ones.** A number with no source is
indistinguishable from a number you made up six months ago, and a seat reading
it cannot tell either. `Source` lets a future run check where it came from;
`As of` lets it notice the figure is four months stale and say so instead of
reasoning from it.

Note the last row. `unknown` is a legitimate, useful entry. Never invent a
placeholder to make the table look finished.

### `DECISION-LOG.md`

```
# Decisions

| Date | Rank | Decision | Why | What would change my mind | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 03 | me | Stopped the $200 starter package | Same effort as the $800 one, and it attracted people who wanted the $800 outcome for $200 | Three people ask for it unprompted in a month | |
```

This is the file that stops you relitigating settled questions every few weeks,
and stops a seat suggesting something you already tried and rejected.

Two columns carry the weight. **"What would change my mind"** is the valuable
one: a decision with no reversal condition is just an opinion with a date on it.

And **`Rank`** records who made the call and under what authority. Yours say
`me`. Once a seat is making decisions it stamps its own rank there, and that
column is what later makes a promotion request checkable rather than a claim.
Leave `Outcome` blank; you fill it in later, honestly, including when you were
wrong.

### `PIPELINE.md`

```
# Pipeline

Stages: **Targeted, Touched, Replied, Quoted, Won, Cold.** Use these words
exactly. A board can count stages it recognises; it cannot count free text.

| Who | What they want | Stage | Last contact | Next step | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ravi Mehta | Full package | Quoted | (date) | Follow up Thursday | me |
| Bright Lane Dental | Not sure yet | Replied | (date) | Send options | me |
```

Be honest about the stage. A pipeline that reads better than reality is the
most expensive file in the folder, because you will make decisions from it.

## Step 3: nothing

That is it. You now have a workspace.

It will look sparse and slightly disappointing. That is correct. Four short
files that are true beat forty pages that are aspirational, and the board reads
these on every single run.

## A note on what you just did

You did not configure anything. There is no account to connect, no integration
to authorise, no data leaving your machine yet. You made a folder and typed
into four files.

Everything from here is pointing an assistant at that folder.

<KeyPoint>Your project is a folder, and your business belongs in it. Four files: goals with floors, metrics with sources, decisions with reversal conditions, and an honest pipeline. Write "unknown" wherever you do not have a number, because an invented number is indistinguishable from a real one to whatever reads it next.</KeyPoint>
