You run something small. You already know AI can help. The problem is that it
only helps on days you remember to ask.

A board fixes the remembering.

## The one-sentence version

**An AI board is a handful of narrow assistants, each with a defined job, that
run on a schedule against files describing your business, and hand you
something to read.**

That is the whole idea. The rest of this path is making it real.

## The four seats

You do not need one clever assistant. You need a few narrow ones, because an
assistant with one job can hold that whole job in its head and does not wander.

| Seat | Reads | Hands you |
|---|---|---|
| **CEO** | Your goals and everything the others produced | What is about to slip, and what you should decide this week |
| **CMO** | Your traffic, content, and what you published | What to publish next, and whether the last thing worked |
| **CFO** | Revenue, expenses, what is due | What is about to lapse and what the month actually looks like |
| **Ops** | Your pipeline and open commitments | What is stuck and who is waiting on you |

Start with one. Most people who start with four end up reading none of them.

## The four files

Here is the part that surprises people: **the board's memory is not in the AI.
It is in four ordinary text files that you own.**

Every run starts with no recollection of the last one. So the files carry
everything forward:

| File | What lives in it |
|---|---|
| `GOALS.md` | What you are trying to move, and the lowest number you would accept |
| `METRICS.md` | The current numbers, and where each one came from |
| `DECISION-LOG.md` | What you decided, and what would make you change your mind |
| `PIPELINE.md` | Named opportunities and where each one honestly stands |

They are plain text. You can read them, edit them in any app, and email them to
your accountant. Nothing is locked inside a tool.

You will create all four in the next lesson.

## The rule that keeps it honest

**If a number is missing, the board must say it is missing.**

This sounds small. It is the difference between a board you can trust and a
board that quietly invents. An assistant that fills a gap with a plausible
figure is worse than one that reports the gap, because the plausible figure
looks exactly like a real one and you will act on it.

You will write this rule into every seat you create.

## What this will not do

Worth saying now rather than after you have built it.

A board produces **analysis, drafts, and reminders, on a schedule**. It does
not make decisions. It does not send the email. It cannot generate demand, and
it will not notice that the one thing blocking you is a conversation you have
been avoiding for a month.

Run a board with nobody acting on it and you get very well organised reports
about a business that is not moving.

The honest promise is narrower and still worth it: **it removes the excuse that
you did not have the analysis.** The work stays yours.

## What you are about to build

Six short lessons from here, you will have a folder, four files, at least one
seat, and a run that happens whether or not you open your laptop.

<KeyPoint>A board is narrow seats, on a schedule, reasoning over files you own. The files are its memory. The schedule is the product. It gives you analysis you would not otherwise have made time for, and nothing more than that.</KeyPoint>
