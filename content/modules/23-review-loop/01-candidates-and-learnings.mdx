A board that runs every week for a year has produced fifty reports. If nothing
carries forward except the reports themselves, you have an archive, not a
system that improves.

This lesson is the smallest mechanism that makes it improve. Two files and one
rule.

## Two files

**`memory/CANDIDATES.md`** — things the board has noticed and proposes you adopt.
It writes here freely, every run, without asking.

**`memory/LEARNINGS.md`** — things you have accepted as true about running this
business. **Only you write here.**

## Why the split is not bureaucracy

Give an agent write access to its own long-term memory and it will, over months,
come to believe things nobody checked. Not through malice: it notices a pattern,
records it as a finding, then reads its own finding next month as established
fact and builds on it.

Afterwards there is no way to tell those beliefs apart from the ones that were
verified. They are the same words in the same file.

The split keeps a human between "I noticed something" and "this is now true."
That is the entire safeguard, and it costs you about a minute a week.

## The two-run rule

**Do not promote a candidate until the same lesson has appeared in two
independent runs.**

One run is an anecdote. A board that saw traffic fall on one Tuesday will
happily conclude Tuesdays are bad. Two independent sightings is a low bar that
removes most of the noise.

One exception, and it is worth stating explicitly: **a safety issue, a
destructive failure, or a demonstrably false instruction gets fixed
immediately.** You do not wait for a second occurrence of something dangerous.

## What a candidate looks like

```markdown
| Proposed | Candidate lesson | Evidence | Runs | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 3 | A correction can go stale. Re-check previous corrections,
          not only original claims | The note saying a command was
          removed; it had come back | 1 | proposed |
```

Four columns doing real work: what was noticed, what makes it credible, how many
times it has been seen, and whether it is still waiting.

**Evidence is the column that matters.** A candidate with no evidence is an
opinion the board had once.

## Where a promoted learning goes

Not just into `LEARNINGS.md`. That file is the record; the *behaviour* changes
somewhere else.

- A lesson about how to run the weekly goes into the **playbook**
- A lesson about what the seat may or may not do goes into the **charter**
- A lesson about which numbers matter goes into **goals**

`LEARNINGS.md` records that you decided it and why. The file that changes
behaviour is the one that gets read at the point the behaviour happens.

Promoting into the record and forgetting to change the playbook is the common
mistake. You end up with a documented lesson the system never applies.

## An empty file is correct

After a month you may have five candidates and nothing promoted.

That is the rule working, not failing. Filling `LEARNINGS.md` early to feel
productive defeats the only thing it is for. The value is that everything in it
survived a second look.

<KeyPoint>The board proposes into candidates, you promote into learnings, and promotion waits for a second independent sighting. The split exists because an agent editing its own memory unsupervised ends up believing things nobody checked. And a promoted lesson has to change a playbook, not just get recorded.</KeyPoint>
