You have spent this course driving Claude Code by hand. You open a session, you
give it context, you review what it does, you ship. That loop is the skill, and
it is the right place to start.

This lesson is about what happens when you stop being the thing that starts the
loop.

## The shift

Everything so far has been **you-initiated**. You decide there is work, you open
a terminal, you supply context, you get output. The ceiling is your attention:
nothing happens on a day you do not sit down.

An operating-company setup inverts that. Work is initiated by a **schedule**,
against **durable files**, using **live data**. You review output rather than
produce it.

Three ingredients make the difference, and none of them are exotic. You already
have all three from earlier modules.

## 1. Seats, not one assistant

A subagent (module 08) with a narrow brief and its own context is a *seat*. The
useful move is to define seats the way a small company would.

A marketing seat reads your traffic and your content queue and proposes what to
publish. A finance seat reads your revenue and flags what is about to lapse. A
product seat reads your issue list and argues about priority.

They are not smarter than one general session. They are **narrower**, which
means each one can hold its whole job in context and none of them wanders.

```markdown
---
name: cmo
description: Reviews traffic and content performance, proposes next week's queue.
---

You are the marketing seat. You read METRICS.md and CONTENT-QUEUE.md.

You propose. You never publish. Every recommendation names the number that
justifies it, and says what you would expect to change if it works.

If a number you need is missing, say it is missing. Do not estimate it.
```

That last line is the one that matters. An agent that fills a gap with a
plausible number is worse than one that reports the gap.

## 2. A schedule

A seat that only runs when you remember it is just a slower version of you. The
scheduling surfaces from module 08 are what make it standing work: a run each
morning that reads yesterday's numbers, a longer run each week that produces a
review.

The cadence is the product. Daily catches drift; weekly catches direction.

## 3. Files the agents read and write

This is the part people skip, and it is the part that makes the whole thing
compound.

Agents are stateless between runs. The files are the memory:

| File | Holds | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| `GOALS.md` | What you are trying to move, and the floor for each | Without it, every run optimises something different |
| `METRICS.md` | The live numbers, with the query that produced them | The agent reasons over facts, not vibes |
| `DECISION-LOG.md` | What you chose and what would reverse it | Stops next month's run relitigating a settled question |
| `PIPELINE.md` | Named opportunities and honest stages | The one file that reveals whether anything is actually moving |

**The metrics file must be generated, never typed.** A number you wrote by hand
in March reads exactly like a current one to an agent in August. It will reason
confidently from it, and nothing in the output will tell you it was stale. Write
the query into the file next to the number so any run can re-derive it.

## What this does not do

Worth saying plainly, because the failure mode is predictable.

This setup produces **analysis, drafts, and reminders on a schedule**. It does
not make decisions. It does not do outreach. It cannot generate demand, and it
will not notice that the one thing blocking you is a conversation you have been
avoiding. A board with no one acting on it produces very well-organised reports
about a business that is not moving.

The honest description is: it removes the excuse that you did not have the
analysis. It does not remove the work.

## Summary

Standing seats, a schedule, and durable files turn a tool you operate into a
system that runs. Every piece is something you already learned: subagents,
scheduling, and CLAUDE.md-style context discipline. The assembly is the new
part, and the discipline that keeps it honest is that every number is read, not
remembered.

<KeyPoint>An operating company is narrow agents on a schedule, reasoning over generated files. The files are the memory and the schedule is the product. It gives you analysis you would not otherwise have made time for. It does not give you decisions, demand, or the courage to send the hard email.</KeyPoint>
