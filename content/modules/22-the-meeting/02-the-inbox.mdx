Your board writes a report. This is how you answer.

Without it, every instruction you have lives in a chat window that the next
scheduled run cannot see. The seat starts each week knowing everything about
your business and nothing about what you said on Tuesday.

## One file, read first

`CHAIRMAN-NOTES.md`. Named for the role rather than the person, because the role
outlives any particular arrangement.

The playbook opens with it, ahead of the charter and ahead of the routine:

```
Step 0. Read CHAIRMAN-NOTES.md and act on anything under New directives
before doing anything else.
```

Order matters. What you left there this week is more current than any routine,
and a report that runs the standard playbook while ignoring your note answers a
question nobody asked.

## The shape

```markdown
## New directives
- Stop proposing paid ads. Not this quarter.
- Look at why enquiries dropped in the second half of the month.

## Direction and vision
- We are narrowing to one service line. Depth over breadth.

## Financial
- Revenue floor moves to $4,000 from next month.

## Promotion decisions
- Granted L2 on the 28th. Earn it retroactively.

## Processed
- (handled items land here with a one-line note)
```

Four inboxes rather than one, because a directive and a vision change need
different handling. A directive is a task; a vision change alters what the seat
thinks it is for.

## The lifecycle, which is the whole trick

**You add → the seat reads it next run → it acts or logs why it cannot → it
moves the item to Processed with a one-line note.**

Without that last step the file becomes an archive that grows forever and gets
read in full every run. With it, the file stays short and you can see at a glance
what is outstanding.

Then a second rule: once a processed item shows up in `DECISION-LOG.md`, delete
it here. The log is the permanent record. This file is a desk, not a filing
cabinet.

## Keep it short, deliberately

This is hot state. Read on every run, so its length is a cost you pay every
single time. Two or three live items is healthy. Fifteen means you are using it
as a notepad.

## When it is not working

**Items sit for weeks.** Usually you are writing things the seat cannot act on:
"think about pricing" is not a task. "Draft three pricing options against the
current numbers and tell me which you would pick" is.

**You never write in it.** Either the reports genuinely need no response, which
is unlikely, or you are replying in a chat window where the next run cannot see
it. That is the failure this file exists to prevent, and it is the common one.

## Say the uncomfortable things here

The most valuable entries are the ones that correct the seat:

```
## New directives
- You recommended the same thing three weeks running. I said no twice.
  Log it as rejected and stop proposing it.
```

Written into the file, that changes behaviour permanently. Said in a chat, it
lasts until the session ends.

<KeyPoint>One file, read before anything else, with four sections and a lifecycle that ends in Processed and then in deletion. Keep it short because it is read every run, and put corrections in writing here rather than in a chat the next run cannot see.</KeyPoint>
