Everything so far has you maintaining `METRICS.md` by hand. That was correct for
week one. This lesson is why it stops being correct, and it is the most
dangerous failure in the whole system.

## The failure

You update your metrics in March. In June you are busy, and you do not.

Your board runs in July. It reads the file. It sees revenue, enquiries, list
size, all present, all formatted correctly. It reasons carefully from them and
produces a thoughtful report recommending something sensible.

Every word of that report is about your business in March.

**Nothing in the output tells you this.** There is no warning, no error, no
missing field. A number typed in March looks exactly like a number pulled this
morning, because it is the same characters in the same cell.

## Why this beats an obviously broken metric

If a number is missing, someone notices. The report says it cannot answer, you
go and find it, the loop closes.

If a number is stale, the report answers confidently, you act on it, and the
loop closes wrongly. The failure mode of a gap is a question. The failure mode
of a stale figure is a decision.

That asymmetry is why this is worth a module rather than a footnote.

## The minimum fix, before connecting anything

You do not need an integration to make this much safer. You need two extra
columns:

```markdown
| Metric          | Value  | Source                          | As of  |
|-----------------|--------|---------------------------------|--------|
| Monthly revenue | $2,640 | Stripe dashboard, month to date | Mar 03 |
| Enquiries       | 4      | Contact form inbox, counted     | Mar 03 |
| Website visits  | unknown| No analytics installed          | -      |
```

Now a run in July can see the date and say so:

> Revenue is dated March 3, four months ago. I am not going to reason about
> revenue trends from it. Refresh it or tell me to proceed anyway.

That is a board doing its job. The number is still hand-typed, but it is no
longer able to lie silently, and the whole problem was the silence.

## Unknown is a real value

Write `unknown` where you do not have a figure. Never a placeholder, never a
guess, never last month's number carried forward.

`unknown` in a metrics table is useful information: it tells the board what it
cannot reason about, and it tells you what is worth going to get. A guess tells
neither of you anything, and it looks identical to a fact.

## What connecting actually buys you

The next lesson connects a real source. Worth being clear about what that
changes, because it is narrower than it sounds.

It does not make your board smarter. It removes one specific failure: the
possibility that a number is older than it appears. That is all, and it is
enough, because that failure produces confident wrong advice rather than
obviously wrong advice.

Everything else in this system works fine on hand-typed numbers, as long as they
carry their date.

<KeyPoint>A stale number reads exactly like a current one, so it produces careful advice about a business that no longer exists, with nothing in the output to warn you. Before connecting anything, put the source and the date beside every figure, and write "unknown" rather than a guess.</KeyPoint>
