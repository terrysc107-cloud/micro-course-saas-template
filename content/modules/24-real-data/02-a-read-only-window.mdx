Connecting a source means your board reads a number instead of trusting one you
wrote down. This lesson is how to do that without handing an autonomous system
the keys to your business.

## Read-only. Always.

Before any mechanics: **the connection may read and must never write.**

A board reasons, drafts, and reports. Nothing in that job requires modifying a
system of record. Not your invoices, not your CRM, not your customer list.

This is not caution for its own sake. A connection that *cannot* write cannot be
talked into writing by a confused run or by instructions hidden in something it
read. The boundary holds without the model's cooperation, which is the only kind
of boundary worth having.

Wherever you are generating credentials, look for the read-only option. Most
tools have one. If a tool only offers full access, that is a reason to hesitate,
not a reason to proceed.

## One source first

The instinct is to connect everything. Resist it.

**Connect the single number you would be most embarrassed to be wrong about.**
For most small businesses that is money: what came in this month.

One source, working, that you trust, beats four half-configured ones. And you
will learn something from the first that changes how you do the rest, which is
wasted if you have already done the rest.

## The mechanics, in one paragraph

Your agent reaches other systems through connectors. Whatever the tool calls
them, the shape is the same: you authorise a connection once, the agent can then
ask that system questions, and it can only do what the credentials permit.

Set it up, then ask the board something you already know the answer to. If it
comes back right, the connection works. If it comes back subtly wrong, you have
just learned something important before you started depending on it.

## Record the query beside the number

The part that matters more than the connection.

When a number arrives from a connection, the `Source` column stops naming a
tool and starts holding the **definition**. Not "Stripe" but what exactly was
counted:

```markdown
| Metric          | Value  | Source                               |
|-----------------|--------|--------------------------------------|
| Paid customers  | 12     | Active subscriptions, excluding
                             cancelled and past-due                |
| Monthly revenue | $2,640 | Sum of succeeded charges, this
                             calendar month, refunds subtracted    |
```

Then one instruction in the playbook: **run these, never improvise a variant.**

This exists because of a real failure. A board reported paid customers as 21 and
rising when the truth was 14 and falling, because a fresh query was written that
omitted the expiry condition. The recorded query was never wrong. It was
bypassable, and it got bypassed.

An improvised query returns a confident number that looks exactly like a correct
one. Writing the definition down is what makes the difference checkable.

## When numbers disagree

They will, the first time. Your hand-typed revenue says one thing, the
connection says another.

**The connected number wins, and you log the disagreement.** Nearly always it
turns out the two were measuring subtly different things: one included refunds,
one counted a different window, one used a different definition of active.

That discovery is worth more than the number. It usually means a decision you
made earlier was based on a figure that meant something other than you thought.

## What still will not be connected

Be honest in your metrics file about what remains manual, because that is where
staleness will now concentrate. Once some numbers refresh themselves and others
do not, the un-refreshed ones get less attention, not more.

Mark them. `updated by hand` in the source column is a small thing that keeps you
honest about which half of your table is living and which is a snapshot.

<KeyPoint>Read-only always, because a connection that cannot write cannot be talked into writing. Connect one source first, the number you would hate to be wrong about. Record how each number was obtained and never improvise a variant. When a connected figure disagrees with your typed one, the connection wins and the disagreement is the finding.</KeyPoint>
