Everything so far has had you keeping four files in a folder. That shape is
correct for week one and it has a shelf life.

Here is the failure, stated plainly so you recognise it before it costs you
anything.

## The failure

Your board writes every week. Decisions accumulate. Meetings accumulate. The
files that were four short pages in September are a small book by December.

Every run reads them. So one of two things happens, and both are bad.

Either the run reads everything, and most of its attention is spent on a
decision from four months ago instead of the thing that changed on Tuesday. Or
it reads part of it and quietly misses what matters most, which is almost always
the most recent part.

**There is no error message.** The board keeps producing confident, articulate
reports. They just get vaguer, and slightly wrong, and you will assume the model
got worse.

## This is not hypothetical

Here is a real board that has run for three months, measured today:

```
DECISION-LOG.md      56 KB
DAILY-PULSE.md       36 KB
CONTENT-QUEUE.md     28 KB
CEO-GOALS.md         24 KB
CHARTER.md           20 KB
```

Over 150KB across the top five files, and that is one seat out of five.

Look at two other files in the same folder: `SHIPPED-2026-06-04.md` and
`SHIPPED-2026-07-04.md`. That is not two documents. That is **one file that got
too big and was split by date** by a person who hit this exact wall and solved it
by hand, in the middle of doing something else.

You are going to hit the same wall. The rest of this module is the shape that
survives it.

## Four different jobs

The mistake is treating "the files" as one thing. They are doing four jobs with
completely different read and write patterns.

| Job | Example | Read | Written | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | the charter | every run | rarely | small, stable |
| **Hot state** | goals, metrics, inbox | every run | constantly | **must stay small** |
| **The record** | decisions, meetings | almost never | every run | grows forever |
| **Learned** | what the system worked out | occasionally | rarely | slow |

Once you see them as four jobs, the fix is obvious: **the things that grow
forever must not be things that are read every time.**

## What that looks like

- Identity and hot state stay in a handful of short files at the top level
- The record moves to a folder of dated files, one per meeting, never one
  growing file
- When a hot file does grow fat, it splits by date and the old part stops being
  read
- One index file says what is where and what to read when

That is the entire architecture. It is not clever. It is just the difference
between a system that works in month one and a system that still works in month
twelve.

## The honest version of the promise

Nobody tells you this part, because a course that ends at "make four files"
looks complete and demos beautifully. It works right up until the point where
you have invested three months of your business's thinking into it.

<KeyPoint>Four flat files work for about six weeks. The things that grow forever must not be read every run, and there is no error when you get this wrong: the board just gets quietly vaguer while sounding exactly as confident. Identity and hot state stay small; the record moves to dated files.</KeyPoint>
